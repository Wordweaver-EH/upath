I've reviewed your critique of the unified approach. While you raise valid concerns, I believe several can be addressed without major architectural changes. Let me debate these points:

1. **Sub-Graph State Isolation**: You're right about encapsulation, but complete isolation would require excessive boilerplate. What if we use a "state slice" pattern where sub-graphs only receive and return their relevant state portion?

2. **Error Boundaries**: The distinction between GraphInterrupt and GraphBubbleUp is important, but isn't our granular error recovery approach (skip failed items) more practical than circuit breakers that could halt the entire pipeline?

3. **Performance & State Growth**: State pruning is critical, but shouldn't we implement it at the node level rather than globally? Each phase could clean up its predecessor's data.

4. **Timeline**: 35-40 days seems excessive. With the hybrid architecture reducing complexity, and if we parallelize development across team members, couldn't we achieve 25 days?

5. **Security/Compliance**: While important, isn't this orthogonal to the LangGraph migration? We could add encryption/audit trails as a separate layer.

What are your thoughts on these counterpoints? Let's refine the approach together.
